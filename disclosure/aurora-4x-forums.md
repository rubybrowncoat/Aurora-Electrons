# Why Aurora Electrons isn't on the Aurora 4x forums

*Written by MatsorBrowncoat, author of Aurora Electrons. Last updated 25 November 2026.*

On 12 November 2026 I posted a thread on the Aurora forums about the anti-modding measures in Aurora v3.x. Within the hour Steve (AuroraSteve), Aurora's developer and the forum's administrator, had deleted it. After a short private exchange my account was suspended and the Aurora Electrons thread was removed. On 25 November the account was deleted, along with all of my posts.

Steve has since described that exchange publicly twice. This page sets out what I posted, what was said in private, and where his description differs from the record. The full texts are at the bottom, so every quotation can be checked.

Steve owns the forum and pays for it, and he decides who posts there. This page doesn't contest that. It exists because people have asked where the Electrons thread went, and because one statement in his account, that I admitted to trying to generate public pressure, is not true.

## The post

After running into failures in my own game, I inspected the relevant code and found that Aurora changes its behaviour when it detects a modification. The post listed five effects:

- The Commander lists come up empty.
- The Economics window does not restore its position, and the Fleet window stops loading early.
- Adding shipyard tasks is blocked.
- Fleet standing orders and automatic commander assignment are suppressed.
- The button for creating an industrial production project becomes unresponsive.

It also said that the suppression of standing orders and commander assignment can begin many turns after a mod is loaded, and that I had not found any of this documented in the release notes for v3.0 to v3.3.

The post left out what triggers each effect and how the detection works. It said that Steve owes nobody mod support and that declining to investigate problems from modified games is reasonable. It asked for three things: documentation of the affected features, an explicit warning or a refusal to run in place of silent degradation, and marking modified saves as unsupported.

For the record, I modify my own copy of Aurora. Those modifications are private, and I have only ever shared them privately. I have no involvement in the public AuroraPatch mods.

## Why a public thread

I considered writing to Steve privately first, and several people I consulted suggested it. Their experience of Steve and their feelings towards him differ from mine. I expected a private message to be shrugged off, or to provoke a gut reaction.

I gave Steve my reasons for posting publicly in the private exchange. I prefer our interactions to be public, and the thread did two things at once: it told players that these measures are active in the current release, and it opened a discussion about them. Steve reads that choice as an attempt to pressure him. That was not my reason, and I never said it was.

## The private exchange

The exchange was four messages on 12 November, two from each of us. The complete text is in [the record](#the-record) below.

Steve's first message said that his view on mods has been clear for years and will not change, and that he had silenced my account “for a couple of weeks”. It also said:

> **AuroraSteve, private message 1**
>
> I am particularly annoyed you would start publishing details on anti-modding measures in the forum.

I replied that the post had not asked him to change his mind, and repeated its three requests.

His second message said a private message “would have been the obvious way” to ask, and continued:

> **AuroraSteve, private message 2**
>
> A grandstanding post on the forum was absolutely the worst possible option, unless your goal was to pressure rather than persuade, and I am sure you knew that.

He then quoted his May post again, including this sentence:

> **AuroraSteve, quoting his post of 7 May 2026**
>
> Ultimately though, anyone running mods is never going to know whether something is being corrupted in the background so, given the time investment in a campaign, the safe option becomes not to use them.

In my second reply I explained why I had not written to him privately. I said in the same sentence that I was being blunt, and apologised for it in advance:

> **MatsorBrowncoat, private message 2**
>
> As far as messaging you directly about it I don’t want to be too blunt so I apologize in advance, but I don’t trust you, and my choice would be to engage with you personally as little as possible, but now this is the only avenue. I prefer our interactions when they are public, and I have always extended my greatest respect. For that reason I chose the best option I saw available that served both as a disclosure to the community that you opted knowingly to not make and as a respectful argument and discussion thread.

## The suspension

After that reply I received the forum's automated suspension email. It gave the end date as 15 November 2026, 21:47, and the reason as “Would not listen to staff feedback”. Below the reason it quoted my sentence about trust, followed by Steve's comment:

> **AuroraSteve, in the suspension email**
>
> OK, well if that is your opinion of me, I see no point in spending my own time and money allowing you to disrupt a generally happy community

My public profile on the forum gave different reasons. A screenshot taken on 13 November shows the suspension reason as “Too combative”, and a separate silence until 23 November 2026, 9:00 am, with the reason “Going against clear forum rules”. When the suspension was temporarily lifted on 19 November, my profile showed the silence running until 26 November. The forum's [community guidelines](https://aurora4x.com/guidelines), as they stood on 19 November 2026, contain no rule about mods or about discussing them. They describe themselves as “not hard and fast rules”, and they also say that moderators “reserve the right to remove any content and any user account for any reason at any time”.

That evening I asked Steve on Discord what feedback was meant, and whether trusting or liking him is a requirement for being on the forums. I received no reply.

The Aurora Electrons thread was removed the same night. By 00:40 another member was asking in a forum chat channel what had happened to it. Apart from the anti-modding thread itself, it was the only content of mine removed at that point; my bug reports stayed on the forum. On 13 November I asked Steve on Discord whether the suspension was now permanent and whether the thread had been deleted or hidden. I received no reply to that either.

On 15 November at 22:55, about an hour after the end time given in the email, I still could not log in. I wrote to Steve on Discord that I assumed the suspension was permanent, and asked for an export of my account data. I had received no email about any change to the suspension.

Steve replied on 19 November that he had “temporarily unsuspended the account for a few days” so that I could download what I needed, and offered to delete my data afterwards if I wanted. I downloaded my posts, thanked him, and asked him to reconsider the full suspension. He did not reply.

On 25 November, some time after 16:00, my account was deleted, along with all of my posts. That includes the bug reports. I had told Steve on 19 November that he did not need to delete the account or its data.

That member's account was then suspended, with the reason shown on their profile as “Consumed disproportionate amounts of staff time”. The suspension has since been lifted; I don't know when.

That account is not mine, and I said so on Discord the same day. I don't know who the member is. I have known, or known of, a handful of people in my area who played Aurora, and I am not in touch with any of them.

A member's city and ISP are details that come from IP address records, which only forum staff can see. The same guidelines tell everyone on the forum: “Don’t harass or grief anyone, impersonate people, or expose their private information.”

### What the account leaves out

- What the thread reported: that Aurora disables features without telling the player, in some cases many turns after a mod was loaded, and that this is not in the release notes. Neither of Steve's messages mentions it.
- That the thread listed effects only and left out the triggers and the detection methods.

## Aurora Electrons now

Aurora Electrons is a general-purpose observation utility for Aurora 4x C#. It reads the Aurora database and shows information about a game, such as production queues, mineral forecasts and colony planning. One command, the map's Save Positions, writes system coordinates back to the database after confirmation; everything else only reads. Steve's chat message describes Electrons as something that “generally helped people”.

The forum thread ran from 8 October to 10 November 2026 and held 16 posts of mine: release announcements, walkthroughs and replies to bug reports. It now returns a 404. Source code, releases and the issue tracker are in [this repository](https://github.com/rubybrowncoat/Aurora-Electrons). I haven't decided yet whether I will keep maintaining it.

<summary>The post: “Anti-modding measures in v3.x: an opinion and a thread” (12 November 2026, 19:28)</summary>

> **Anti-modding measures in v3.x: an opinion and a thread**
>
> @AuroraSteve, I'd like to open a frank discussion about the anti-modding measures themselves with you and the community, while avoiding reopening disputes about particular modders and past disagreements.
>
> Aurora is your hobby project, distributed free. You owe nobody mod support, compatibility, source code, or features you don't want to build, and investigating problems introduced by someone else's modifications is a cost you shouldn't have to bear if you don't want to. What I want to discuss is much narrower: the difference between declining to support a modified game and making it malfunction when a modification is detected. After running into failures in-game, I inspected the relevant code.
>
> **What stops working**
>
> When the game detects a modification, it changes its own behaviour in these ways:
>
> - The Commander lists come up empty.
> - The Economics window does not restore its position, and the Fleet window stops loading early.
> - Adding shipyard tasks is blocked.
> - Fleet standing orders and automatic commander assignment are suppressed.
> - The button for creating an industrial production project becomes unresponsive.
>
> I've left out what triggers each effect and how the detection works, so that this post doesn't double as a guide to bypass them.
>
> The suppression of standing orders and commander assignment also doesn't necessarily begin when a mod is loaded. It can switch on at some later turn, so automation can work for a while and then stop, without the player having changed anything.
>
> Detecting a modification does not establish that standing orders or commander assignment have been changed or broken; an interface-only modification can trigger restrictions on unrelated gameplay. And those two systems exist so that players don't have to supervise every action. Suppressing them leaves expected tasks undone while the player keeps making decisions on the assumption that automation is running.
>
> **"Then don't use mods"**
>
> Not using mods avoids these effects, but explaining how to avoid a penalty doesn't establish that the penalty is warranted.
>
> Silent penalties work against the support goal: a player who doesn't know why standing orders stopped can only report it as a bug, and that report is harder to diagnose than one from a save marked as modified. A clear refusal to proceed would tell players where they stood. Allowing play to continue while silently suppressing automation gives them just another potential explanation for unexpected behaviour: not an ordinary bug, their own mistake, or a mod incompatibility, but a restriction imposed by the original game. The delayed onset makes that harder still, because the restriction can begin long after the mod was installed and used. A player can accept responsibility for failures their modifications cause; failures introduced to discourage those modifications are a different matter.
>
> **Support and deterrence**
>
> Your [bug-reporting instructions for v3.2.0](https://aurora4x.com/t/bug-reporting-for-v3-2-0/1334) already require players using mods to reproduce problems in the unmodified game. That is a completely reasonable boundary, and the detections you already make could help you reinforce it: mark a save as having been used in a detected modified environment, retain that history after the mod is removed, and decline to investigate that save unless the issue can be reproduced independently in an unmodified game. This wouldn't catch every modification or stop determined tampering, and it would take development work of its own. But it would make the unsupported status explicit and give you an additional basis for declining those reports.
>
> These alternatives are not equivalent, and I don't want to pretend they are. Gameplay penalties may discourage modding altogether, which means fewer modified installations and possibly fewer support requests. But that extra deterrence falls on players who would never submit a mod-related bug report. The people able to write executable mods are also the people best placed to find and remove these checks, so the penalties land mostly on players who only installed someone else's mod. Avoiding unnecessary support work is legitimate; impairing the campaigns of people who ask for no support goes further than that goal requires. An escalating set of checks and countermeasures also costs development time and adds places for bugs to appear, and that belongs in the comparison too.
>
> v3.0 also removed much of the reason to use AuroraPatch in the first place, by bringing colours and themes into AuroraConfig, and I appreciate that. With less demand for mods, there is less for the penalties to deter.
>
> **Creative control**
>
> Your [Mods and v3.0](https://aurora4x.com/t/mods-and-v3-0/510) post also explained that substantial changes to your personal creation make you uncomfortable, while distinguishing those from aesthetic changes, so I recognise that the objection isn't only about maintenance.
>
> But protecting the official game and controlling someone's private experience are different things. A local interface change or an experiment with a mechanic does not, by itself, redistribute your work, claim your endorsement, or oblige you to provide support. You can disapprove, decline to support it, and ask people not to do it. Whether that disapproval justifies impairing unrelated functionality is a separate question, and creative control alone doesn't settle it.
>
> **Risk and documentation**
>
> In that post you also acknowledged that "its possible I could cause some save corruption by accident" and contemplated "more stealthy" countermeasures if the initial ones were circumvented. I'm not claiming these checks have corrupted a save, or that corruption was the intent. But accepting a risk to campaign integrity as a way of enforcing an anti-modding policy requires a justification that matches what players could lose. Even without file corruption, missed automation can affect a campaign before the player discovers what happened. It feels like a punishment.
>
> You announced the general policy publicly in May, but I haven't found these specific restrictions documented in the release notes for [v3.0](https://aurora4x.com/t/aurora-v3-0-0-patch-notes-november-1st-2026/49), [v3.1](https://aurora4x.com/t/aurora-v3-1-0-patch-notes-3rd-november-2026/1268), [v3.2](https://aurora4x.com/t/aurora-v3-2-0-patch-notes-november-5th-2026/1302/1) or [v3.3](https://aurora4x.com/t/aurora-v3-3-0-patch-notes-november-10th-2026/1335). An advance warning that modified games may malfunction isn't the same as telling players which systems are now disabled and under what conditions, and explaining the affected features and what happens after a mod is removed wouldn't require publishing bypass instructions.
>
> **But what am I asking, exactly?**
>
> Aurora and the people who play it mean a great deal to me, and I appreciate the work you share with all of us. That appreciation doesn't require agreement with every decision, just as my disagreement doesn't oblige you to accommodate my preferences.
>
> But I would ask you to:
>
> 1. Document the affected features, the triggering conditions, and any persistent consequences.
> 2. Remove silent gameplay degradation and make detection explicit. If letting a modified session continue is unacceptable to you, a clear refusal to proceed is still preferable to hidden restrictions.
> 3. Use detection to identify unsupported saves, while keeping the requirement that reported bugs be reproduced in an unmodified game.
>
> That leaves me with three questions:
>
> - What support problem would remain after rejecting flagged saves and requiring a clean reproduction, and how much of it would gameplay penalties actually prevent?
> - What does silently disabling automation achieve beyond deterring modifications through the possibility of an unreliable campaign, and why is that worth the cost to players who would never ask you to support their mods?
> - Where do you draw the line between preserving your vision for the game and enforcing that vision on someone's private experience?
>
> Would you consider replacing these penalties with explicit warnings and unsupported-save marking, even while maintaining your opposition to executable mods?
